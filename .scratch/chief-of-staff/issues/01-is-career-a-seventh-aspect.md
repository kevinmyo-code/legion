# Is career a seventh aspect, and what falsifiable data would anchor it

Charted 2026-09-12 from Kevin's own framing: *"alfred will be the executive of my estate, my chief of
staff."* See `.scratch/chief-of-staff/map.md` for what already exists - most of the advisor
machinery does - and for why this one is a gap rather than a rebuild.

## Why this is a ruling, not a feature

CLAUDE.md §1 names six aspects and says plainly: *"Six domains, and the list is a decision - a
seventh is a ruling, not a refactor."* There is no career code anywhere in the tree. Kevin asked for
career advice explicitly, so this is his to decide rather than something to slide in under an
existing aspect.

## The hard part, and it is not the schema

**Career is the aspect with the least falsifiable data**, and that collides with a standing rule.
CLAUDE.md §7: *"memory stays anchored to external falsifiable facts... A persona may be fond of the
driver; it may not invent unfalsifiable history with them."*

Every other aspect has an external anchor that can contradict the model:

| Aspect | What can prove it wrong |
|---|---|
| Fleet | An OBD dongle, an odometer |
| Ledger | A statement that prints its own total |
| Pantry | A receipt |
| Bio | A logged set, a weight, a meal |
| Dates | A calendar row with a real timestamp |

Career has **applications, interviews, offers, salary, titles, dates** - all real and falsifiable if
recorded, and all invisible unless Kevin records them. So the ticket's real question is not "should
there be a career aspect" but **"what would LEGION actually hold, such that advice about it is
anchored rather than invented?"**

An aspect whose only input is conversation is a model reasoning about a man's life from memory, which
is the failure mode §7 exists to prevent.

## What the decision must settle

1. **Seventh aspect, or a slice of an existing one?** Applications-and-deadlines is shaped like LOG;
   salary is shaped like CRED.
2. **What is recorded.** The minimum that makes advice falsifiable rather than vibes.
3. **What it is never allowed to say.** `CredPlaybook` drew the finance line well; the career
   equivalent needs the same treatment before a playbook is written, not after.
