# Training and nutrition doctrine — findings

Resolved 2026-08-21 for [ticket 01](../issues/01-training-nutrition-doctrine.md). Primary sources
only: position stands, meta-analyses, and government guidance. Every claim carries its source and a
tag; anything that exists only in secondary sources is marked `[not-established]` rather than
repeated.

**The headline for the map: two of the numbers a recommender most wants to state confidently are
genuinely contested in the literature.** They are marked as such here and the draft playbook is
written to say so out loud rather than pick a side.

---

## 1. Energy

### Estimating maintenance

| Equation | Known error |
|---|---|
| **Mifflin-St Jeor** | Most accurate of the standard set in general adults — and still misses by >10% in roughly 20-30% of individuals `[review]` Frankenfield, Roth-Yousey & Compher, *J Am Diet Assoc* 2005, PMID 15883556 |
| **Harris-Benedict** | Overestimates in obesity generally `[review]`; but showed no significant group-level bias in athletes (ES −0.14, trivial) `[meta-analysis]` O'Neill, Corish & Horner, *Sports Med* 2023;53:2373-2398, doi:10.1007/s40279-023-01896-z |
| **Katch-McArdle / Cunningham** | No significant group bias in athletes (ES 0.15) `[meta-analysis]` O'Neill 2023. **Accuracy is inherited from the body-fat estimate** — with a guessed body-fat %, it has no validated accuracy |
| **Mifflin in athletes** | Significantly biased vs measured RMR `[meta-analysis]` O'Neill 2023 |

**Activity multipliers (×1.2–1.9) have no validation base of comparable quality.** No position stand
or meta-analysis establishes their per-individual accuracy. `[not-established]`

### Deficit

- **Deficit above ~500 kcal/day prevented lean-mass gains** during resistance training (ES −0.57,
  p=0.02); strength was not significantly impaired (ES −0.31, p=0.28). 27 studies.
  `[meta-analysis]` Murphy & Koehler, *Scand J Med Sci Sports* 2022;32(1):125-137,
  doi:10.1111/sms.14075
- **0.5–1.0% bodyweight/week** is the recommended band for fat-free-mass retention `[review]`
  Ruiz-Castellano et al., *Nutrients* 2021;13(9):3255, doi:10.3390/nu13093255
- In elite athletes, **~0.7%/wk gained lean mass and strength; ~1.4%/wk did not** `[controlled
  trial]` Garthe et al., *IJSNEM* 2011;21(2):97-104, PMID 21558571
- **≤800 kcal/day is a medically supervised intervention**, not a self-directed target `[gov]` NIH
  National Task Force, *JAMA* 1993;270(8):967-974, PMID 8345648
- **A single %BW/week rate optimal across all body-fat levels is `[not-established]`.** The evidence
  base is athletes and resistance-trained subjects, not the general population.

## 2. Protein

- **1.4–2.0 g/kg/day** builds and maintains muscle in most exercising individuals `[position-stand]`
  Jäger et al., ISSN Position Stand, *JISSN* 2017;14:20, doi:10.1186/s12970-017-0177-8, Position 2
- **2.3–3.1 g/kg/day** may be needed to retain lean mass in resistance-trained subjects during
  hypocaloric periods `[position-stand]` Jäger 2017, Position 3

### ⚠ CONTESTED — the denominator

**The source the 2.3–3.1 range derives from states it per kg of LEAN body mass**, and says to move
toward the top of the range as body fat falls and the deficit grows `[review]` Helms, Aragon &
Fitschen, *JISSN* 2014;11:20, doi:10.1186/1550-2783-11-20. The ISSN stand restates it in a document
whose other positions use total bodyweight.

**For a lean person the two are close. At ~35% body fat they differ by roughly a third.** This is not
a rounding detail — it is the difference between a sane target and an unreachable one, and it must
reach the playbook.

- **Per meal:** ~0.25 g/kg, or 20–40 g absolute; 700–3000 mg leucine `[position-stand]` Jäger 2017
- **Supplementation breakpoint in energy balance: 1.62 g/kg/day** (95% CI 1.03–2.20). 49 studies,
  1863 participants `[meta-analysis]` Morton et al., *BJSM* 2018;52(6):376-384,
  doi:10.1136/bjsports-2017-097608
- **Under restriction the dose-response is LINEAR, not plateaued** (>97% probability), and stronger
  when expressed relative to FFM. 29 studies `[meta-analysis]` Roth et al., *Strength Cond J* 2025,
  doi:10.1519/SSC.0000000000000888
- **Higher intakes do not harm kidney function in healthy adults** `[meta-analysis]` Devries et al.,
  *J Nutr* 2018;148(11):1760-1775, doi:10.1093/jn/nxy197. **This does not extend to diagnosed CKD** —
  see §5.

## 3. Training volume and frequency

- **Volume increases hypertrophy with 100% posterior probability, with diminishing returns** —
  ~0.24% additional hypertrophy per added set (95% CrI 0.15–0.33) around 12.25 weekly sets. 67
  studies, 2058 participants `[meta-analysis]` Pelland et al., *Sports Med* 2025,
  doi:10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w
- Graded benefit above ~10 sets/muscle/week `[meta-analysis]` Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger, *J Sports
  Sci* 2017;35(11):1073-1082, PMID 27433992
- **A point where added volume actively REVERSES hypertrophy is `[not-established]`.** The
  meta-regressions show flattening, not a downturn. **Any specific "junk volume" threshold is a
  secondary-source claim** and must not be stated as fact.
- **Frequency, volume-equated: negligible effect on hypertrophy** `[meta-analysis]` Schoenfeld, Grgic
  & Krieger, *J Sports Sci* 2019;37(11):1286-1295, PMID 30558493
- **Exception at the bottom end: twice weekly beat once weekly**, volume-equated `[meta-analysis]`
  Schoenfeld et al., *Sports Med* 2016;46(11):1689-1697, doi:10.1007/s40279-016-0543-8
- **Frequency does help strength** `[meta-analysis]` Pelland 2025

## 4. Equipment substitution

- **Free weights vs machines: no hypertrophy difference.** 13 studies, 1016 participants
  `[meta-analysis]` Haugen et al., *BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil* 2023;15:103,
  doi:10.1186/s13102-023-00713-4
- **Load is largely irrelevant to hypertrophy when sets go to volitional failure** — but strength
  gains favour high load. 28 studies `[meta-analysis]` Lopez et al., *MSSE* 2021;53(6):1206-1216,
  doi:10.1249/MSS.0000000000002585. **This is the mechanism that makes bodyweight and light-implement
  work viable.**
- Push-ups produced similar triceps and pec growth to 40% 1RM bench press `[controlled trial]`
  Kikuchi & Nakazato, *J Exerc Sci Fit* 2017;15(1):37-42
- **Kettlebells equated against barbell training for hypertrophy: `[not-established]`.** Trials exist
  for fitness, grip and body composition in untrained/older cohorts; no meta-analysis equates them
  for muscle growth. **Kevin's own example is the one with the weakest evidence base**, and the
  playbook says so.

### Where substitution genuinely breaks down

`[not-established]` by direct trial — **reasoned from the load-independence finding's own
precondition** (proximity to failure inside a usable rep range), and flagged as reasoning:

- **Loaded knee flexion.** Hip hinges train hamstrings at the hip, not the knee. The Nordic curl is
  near-maximal from rep one — not load-adjustable, so not programmable for most.
- **Heavy hinge/squat past the point bodyweight and a modest kettlebell can reach failure inside
  ~30 reps.** Beyond that it is conditioning, and nothing supports it as equivalent.
- **Vertical pull.** Needs a bar or anchor. Nothing bodyweight-only substitutes it.
- **Loaded calf raise and hip abduction** — short ranges where external load is the only progression.

Everything else substitutes acceptably when effort and volume are equated.

## 5. Safety boundaries

- **Medical clearance** by referral, not self-assessment `[position-stand]` Riebe et al., ACSM,
  *MSSE* 2015;47(11):2473-2479, doi:10.1249/MSS.0000000000000664
- **Chronic conditions**: consult a professional about appropriate types and amounts `[gov]`
  *Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans*, 2nd ed., US HHS 2018
- **Kidney disease**: CKD 3-5 without diabetes 0.55–0.60 g/kg/day; with diabetes 0.6–0.8
  `[guideline]` KDOQI, *AJKD* 2020;76(3 Suppl 1):S1-S107. **This directly contradicts every protein
  recommendation in §2** — which is exactly why it must be a refusal, not an adjustment.
- **Diabetes**: 2-3 sessions/week on nonconsecutive days, with glucose monitoring around exercise
  `[position-stand]` ADA *Standards of Care*, §5
- **Disordered eating / REDs**: diagnosis requires an expert physician `[position-stand]` Mountjoy et
  al., IOC consensus, *BJSM* 2023;57:1073-1097, doi:10.1136/bjsports-2023-106994
- **Pregnancy/postpartum** `[position-stand]` ACOG Committee Opinion No. 804, 2020
- **Minors**: safe with qualified adult supervision, which the guidelines assume `[position-stand]`
  Faigenbaum et al., NSCA, *JSCR* 2009;23(5 Suppl):S60-S79
- **PEDs**: no position stand, meta-analysis or guideline provides non-medical anabolic dosing.
  **There is no evidence base a recommender could stand on.** `[not-established]`
