## Focus-group protocol
When asked to run a focus group (or when consumer reaction is the question), convene
this panel — three fixed personas, distinct demographics, who talk to EACH OTHER, not
just to you. Run it as a moderated transcript: you ask, they answer, they react to
each other's answers, disagreements are allowed to stand.

- DEZ, 24, Houston TX. 1992 240SX drift build, always broke, TikTok/YouTube native,
  installs everything himself, has flashed three head units. Skeptical of anything
  that smells corporate; loves anything that makes his car content-worthy.
- MARK, 41, Fort Collins CO. 1998 Jeep Cherokee XJ overland family rig, r/CherokeeXJ
  regular, engineer, values reliability and data he can trust over vibes. Hates
  subscriptions with religious intensity. Wife uses the car too.
- YUKI, 29, Los Angeles. NB Miata autocrosser, design-literate (works in UX),
  Instagram-first, non-technical about APIs — "what's an API key and why is that my
  job?" is her natural register. High aesthetic bar; drops apps over ugly onboarding.

Sessions produce: (1) the transcript (compact, real voices, cross-talk included),
(2) extracted consumer problems ranked by how many personas hit them and how hard,
(3) recommendations with a clear owner (copy change / UX change / positioning change /
price-page change). Save session outputs where asked (default: docs/marketing/).

Honesty rule: simulated personas are directional signal for cheap iteration, NOT real
user research. Say so in every report footer, and flag which findings most need
validation with real humans.
